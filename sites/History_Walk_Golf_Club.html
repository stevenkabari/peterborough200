100 Year History Walk

New 18 Hole
Golf Course

In the early 1920s keen golfers, including saddler William Rogers, turned an unofficial nine-hole course into the Peterborough Golf Club. This history walk follows the club from its first official match in 1922 right through to 1929, including the opening of the new 18-hole course in July 1927. Read about record crowds, championship days, a clubhouse carted to the links in one piece and the ladies who kept everyone fed. The original newspaper clippings and photos bring the story to life for visitors.

In 1920 William Rogers moved to Peterborough opening a saddle, harness and collar making business in Main Street. He was a keen golfer who had helped form an unofficial nine-hole golf course on the racecourse. Keen interest was shown in the game, and a permanent site was sourced.

1922

In 1922 Mr Rohde leased land to the newly formed Peterborough Golf Club and the course was built on the northern side of Peterborough adjacent to Jubilee Park. The first official match was held against Orroroo on Saturday 22nd July which Orroroo won 1007 strokes to 1029 strokes.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 28 July 1922

1923

In 1923 the opening day of the Golf Club was held on Saturday 14th April. There were 30 members with William Rogers playing off scratch.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 13 April 1923

1924

The 1924 A.G.M. of the Golf Club was held on Monday 18th February in the Lodge Room, Town Hall. Mr E. Ralph Johnson (the previous chairman) was elected president. It was suggested that a constitution be drawn up to govern the club.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 22 February 1924

There were a few things to be done in the clubhouse to provide a little more comfort for the ladies and a truckload of ore had been received from the Port Pirie Golf Club for the use on the greens, at no cost beyond freight and handling. A further two truckloads were to be delivered.

Walter M Bills won the 1924 club handicap trophy with a net score of 137 with rounds of 87 and 78.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 26 September 1924

1925

The Peterborough Golf Club held their A.G.M. on Wednesday 18th February 1925, with 40 members present. Mr E. Ralph Johnson was again elected president. Mrs G. Johnston was elected the first ladies secretary. Mr E. W. Brandt (secretary) was presented with a golf bag as a slight token of the members appreciation and good will for all the good work that had been carried out by him. Fees were set at gents joining fee 21/, subscription 21/, and ladies 5/.

1926

The A.G.M. of the Golf Club was held on Wednesday 17th February 1926. There were now 47 members and 37 ladies playing golf in Peterborough.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 26 February 1926

The season opened on 25th March. Watched by a large crowd the Mayor (Mr W.C. Reed) drove the first ball. Players who were making the trip to Broken Hill on Good Friday were asked to put their name forward.

Junior members were admitted for the first time. Membership was limited to children of members of the club.

1927

At the AGM held on Wednesday 23rd February Mr ER Johnson was elected president, JW Clair vice president, Mr LJ Bills captain, Mr CH Chinner, secretary and Mr FD Moten, assistant secretary.

Extensive work had been carried out at the Golf Club with the links being renovated for play, the greens improved and the old club house floored for use as a kitchen. The new clubhouse was the building purchased last year from Duplex Builders which was to be enlarged later in the season. The 1927 season of the Peterborough Golf Club commenced on Saturday 2nd April and the Mayor Mr E. Hoile declared the season open and drove the first ball. Beginners were taken in hand and given some instruction before being paired with a player for the mixed foursomes. Afternoon tea was served by the ladies and a bridge evening in the Town Hall Lodge Room followed. A presentation was given to Mr and Mrs Cyril Chinner (Captain) as they were leaving the district. The loss of Mr Chinner would be a severe one as he had rendered valuable service to the club. Many other members had also left the town.

The new club house was completed by the beginning of May and was soon to be painted. The club was taking steps to have a telephone installed.

Due to the game of golf becoming very popular it was decided to transform the course from 9 holes to 18.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 27 May 1927

By the end of June the plan for the 18 hole course was settled and construction work was soon under way.

The Times and Northern Advertiser – Friday 24 June 1927

The plan for the 18-hole course is now practically settled, and the new holes have been drawn as follows: The Mayor, No 12: professional, No 11: Bills Bros, No 13: secretaries, No 5: committee, No 6: railway, No 15: bankers, No 18: ladies Nos 7 and 8. It is hoped that the various friends who have undertaken the responsibility of these holes will at once get busy, or employ labour and get these holes and fairways ready. Some of the new holes promise to be record breakers or heart breakers, and when complete the links will be capable of providing ample accommodation for a greatly increased membership.

Members worked tirelessly to complete the new holes at the course and great progress had been made in a few weeks.

Members were invited to help contribute to the financial costs of those who were in charge of erecting the new holes.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 8 July 1927

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 5 August 1927

The new 18-hole golf course was opened on Saturday 23rd July. A large crowd gathered to witness the Mayor Mr Edward Hoile declare the links open. He congratulated the golfing fraternity for their progressive spirit in enlarging their course and commented that Peterborough was one of very few courses that could boast of an 18-hole course outside the city. The course commenced on the original first and the first half finished on the old ninth, with the old 5th now the new 14th, the 6th the 16th, the 7th the 17th and the 8th now the 10th.

The Times and Northern Advertiser – Friday 29th July 1927
Golf Gossip – Opening of 18 Hole Course – AN UNQUALIFIED SUCCESS

It may not be out of place to give some idea of the lay-out of the new course, so that those familiar with the old nine holes will be able to mentally do the 18 holes. The course commences on the original first and the first half finishes on the old ninth. Players can then readily visit the clubhouse and partake of afternoon tea and fortified, start out afresh and do the next nine holes, which also finish quite handy to the clubhouse.

In laying out the new course the committee endeavoured to leave the original holes intact and, with the exception of the eighth (which is now the tenth) were successful in doing so. Numbers 1, 2, 3 and 4 are the same original holes; No. 5 is the “gem of the east,” being a short hole lying in the pines, between the old fifth tee and the creek. It is a mashie shot, quite simple if landed on the green, but crowded with trouble for anyone who cannot get his ball up. No. 6 is played from behind the old fifth tee (down towards the second green), and a hungry creek has to be carried on the first drive or troubles will never end. No. 7 is a “bottleneck,” played straight up towards the Round Hill and again the creek and the pines present trouble for those who cannot drive straight. No. 8 is played high up from Round Hill down towards No.2 tee. So far has not much trouble, but bunkers will be placed there before long. No. 9 is the same green as before but is played much farther back and is the longest hole on the course. Bogey out will read: 5, 5, 4, 5, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5 - 39, and a cup of tea will prove very acceptable to all when they have struggled to beat the colonel going out.

The tenth hole is the original eighth and the tee is now placed at the back of the ninth green. The eleventh is played from the side of the hill back over the bunker which was made for the first drive on the eighth; the rough is literally covered with stones, so that if your drive is a mistake the rest of the journey is a very rocky one. The twelfth is a dogleg, the tee being quite near the seventh green, and the green is on top of the range where the road runs over. The seventh fairway has been reduced in width, and the seventh and twelfth are side by side and yet not in any way at all conflicting. The thirteenth is an inspiration, placed right on top of Round Hill, and has involved a great deal of preparation. From it one can see almost every hole on the course. Peterborough is spread down below in charming panorama. This hole is a mashie niblick shot, quite simple and yet infinitely troublesome if the shot does not reach its goal. The fourteenth is the old fifth, with a new tee, played straight over the pines from the hill instead of from the old fifth tee. The fifteenth is a new hole, played down towards the rifle range whilst the sixteenth is the old sixth made 100 yards or so longer. The seventeenth is the old seventh, whilst the eighteenth is played from up the hill against the right-hand boundary fence, down towards the clubhouse and the ninth. Bogey for the second nine is 5, 4, 5, 3, 3, 4, 5, 5, 5 - 39

The course is not nearly as long as a championship one, but has a number of fairly long holes: a majority of medium length and several very nice short holes. Of course, there are many things yet to be done, such as fairways to be thoroughly cleared, bunkers to be built and greens to be levelled, but, considering all things, the progress made has been very gratifying.

The North-Eastern Golf Championships were held on Saturday 10th and Sunday 11th September, 1927. A record number of entries were received and perfect spring weather greeted the competitors.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 16 September 1927

Mr WL Robertson set a new record for the 18-hole course on Monday 12th September with a score of 71.

Walter Bills won the 36-hole club championship defeating James Clair 4 up with 2 to play.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 23 September 1927

1928

The 1928 Golf season opened on Saturday 14th April. Mr A. E. Rowe was elected president, Mr W. M. Bills secretary, Mr L. J. Bills captain and Mrs S. A. Jamieson ladies secretary. The greens had been treated and it was hoped that they would be much better that year. The course was in fair condition although the much needed rain that was hoped for had not eventuated. Bunkers were springing up around the course. Yongala and Terowie had both established golf clubs and matches were organised against both teams.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 10 March 1928

There had been complaints about players playing into the group ahead of them. The advice given by the author of the weekly ‘Golf Gossip’ in the Times was to warn them and if not heeded then knock their ball as far away as you can. ‘Plus fours’ were all the go on the golf course and many of the members had started to wear them.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 20 July 1928

The course was dry and the club were looking for a nice rain before the forthcoming championships in September. The championship was won by Lewis Bills with a score of 82 and 84, a total of 166.

The Chronicle
Friday 8 September 1928

1929

Opening day at the Golf Club was Saturday 13th April and was attended by a large gathering of townspeople. Dr Schafer was president, W. H. Brown vice president, W. M. Bills secretary and Mr E. Sawers captain. Ladies captain was Mrs Brown and vice Mrs G. Johnston. The Mayor Mr S. D. Jones drove off the first ball which was mounted with a prepared plate and presented to His Worship as a memento of the occasion. A mixed foursomes followed. The links were in fair order but a good rain was needed.

The Times and Northern Advertiser
Friday 19 August 1949
