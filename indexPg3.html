St Joseph's
Catholic School

Discover how a small chapel school grew into St Joseph's Catholic School in Peterborough. It began in 1878 with land donated by John McInerney and a church that doubled as a classroom. Over the years came new churches, picnics, concerts and the arrival of the Sisters of St Joseph in 1897. A memorial school opened free of debt in 1926, later adding a convent, boarding school and summer school for outback students. Completely refurbished in 1988, and further additions have followed ever since.

The tank
on Government Road

Water has shaped Peterborough since the town began in 1876. Early settlers relied on wells, tanks and carted water, while dry summers and salty supplies kept hopes for a permanent scheme alive. After decades of meetings, tests and petitions, a well and storage tank were built in 1927, though shortages soon followed. Ideas of piping in Murray River water finally succeeded in 1958. Follow the story of the tank that stands on Government Road, and see how determined locals kept the taps running.

Town Hall

Step inside the story of Peterborough Town Hall, which began life in 1884 as the Institute. Over the years the building grew through additions, court disputes, and a grand opening by the Governor in 1894. Noise from the railway yards led to plans for a new hall, and after ballots, loans and long debate, today's hall opened in 1927. It has hosted thousands of balls, weddings, concerts and community meetings ever since, and remains one of the most majestic halls in regional Australia.

Y.M.C.A.

Step back to 1925 and discover how the Y.M.C.A. came to Peterborough. After returned soldiers and railway men backed the idea, a site near the town hall was provided and a gala day marked the laying of the foundation stone in 1926. The modern two storey building offered a gymnasium, billiard room, reception hall and rooms for young men away from home. It opened debt free in May 1927, thanks to generous local donations and strong support from the railways and nearby Burra.
